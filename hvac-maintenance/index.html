The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

Filters, a clear outdoor unit, open registers, and a quick look at the drain are the homeowner's share of maintenance, and they matter. Everything beyond that needs a meter. Failures that leave you without heat or air rarely look like anything: an 80 percent capacitor looks like a healthy one, and a low charge is visible only in superheat and subcooling. A crack in the heat exchanger, a lazy flame signal, a pressure switch near its limit, or an amp draw drifting toward the breaker rating all stay hidden until someone measures them, and each is cheaper to fix a season early.

With gas equipment, the stakes go beyond cost. Burners, flame proving, venting, and draft should be verified with instruments every year, because when they fail, the result can be combustion gases in your living space, not just a chilly house. That is why our experienced technicians treat safety testing as the center of every fall visit rather than an add-on. Maintenance Tips for HVAC Maintenance

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Clear the condensate drain early in cooling season, before algae has a chance to grow
  • Keep supply and return registers open in every room, even the ones you rarely use
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
  • Change the filter every one to three months; it is the single highest-value habit there is
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of vegetation and rinse the coil gently each spring
  • Get your tune-ups on the calendar during mild weather, well before the rush
  • Check carbon monoxide alarms monthly in any home with fuel-burning appliances
